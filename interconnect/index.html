FERC Rejects PJM Large Load Plan, Forcing Queue Strategy Rethink

FERC last week rejected the bulk of PJM's large load interconnection proposal, finding that key provisions intrude on state utility jurisdiction and raise discrimination concerns by effectively creating a preferential pathway for high-demand customers seeking dedicated grid capacity. The ruling lands at a particularly consequential moment. PJM already operates one of the most congested interconnection queues in the country, and the rejected framework had been widely anticipated as a potential relief valve for hyperscale and data center developers racing to secure grid access in the Mid-Atlantic and Midwest markets. That relief valve is now closed, at least in its proposed form.

The market mechanics shift meaningfully as a result. Key elements of PJM's proposal, including interim service and 'bring your own new capacity' rules, would have allowed large loads to connect to the grid without matching new capacity if they agreed to be curtailed ahead of grid emergencies.

Our read: Without that mechanism, data center developers fall back into the standard queue process. Developers who structured site acquisition or capital deployment timelines around an expedited large load pathway are now working with a materially different set of assumptions. For site selection and queue strategy, the practical implication is heightened scrutiny on queue position and interconnection study risk. Projects advanced specifically to leverage the large load framework may require reassessment. Developers should treat any successor proposal from PJM with caution; the timing of any revised filing and its approval is not yet known. Developers underwriting projects on the premise of regulatory accommodation in queue processes should treat that premise as a risk factor, not a baseline.
