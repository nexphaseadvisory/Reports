01  The Bottom Line
Held for review
PJM is the center of gravity today. The backstop procurement delay, 169 GW entering Phase 1 of the new queue process, Pennsylvania's executive order on self-supply, and a $10 billion BTM campus at a retired coal site all point in the same direction: the grid cannot absorb what is coming, developers who control their own power supply are being rewarded, and the capacity cost pressure is not going away regardless of how long FERC takes to sort out the backstop rules. Developers without a clear interconnection right or a credible BTM strategy are already behind.

02  Signal of the Day
PJM
Transmission617 projects totaling ~169 GW enter Phase 1 of PJM's new first-ready, first-served interconnection process; demand expected to grow 70 GW by 2038.The Read▾
PJM · 2026-09-30
617 projects and roughly 169 GW entering Phase 1 of PJM's first-ready, first-served process is the number that frames everything else happening in PJM right now. PJM's own load forecast shows 70 GW of demand growth by 2038. The math does not close. Most of those 169 GW will not be built, and the study process will sort winners from losers over the next several years, but the volume entering the queue confirms that the constraint is not developer appetite. It is study capacity, transmission headroom, and capital discipline on the developer side. Whoever holds a position that already cleared the prior cluster is sitting on the most valuable interconnection asset in North America.

Data Center Frontier · 2026-10-01
The Amazon-Talen case at Susquehanna is the precedent that every BTM and co-location developer in PJM is actually being priced off of right now. FERC and PJM are converging on a hybrid-grid model: you can supply your own power, but your grid obligations will reflect what the system has to carry for you during an outage, not just your net metered demand. That is a materially larger financial exposure than most BTM business plans have modeled, and it is the number that needs to be stress-tested before any campus breaks ground.

01SignalMeta signed a large solar PPA in Texas while facing scrutiny for simultaneously powering some data centers with natural gas.Held for reviewThe Read▾
Meta
Meta signing a large Texas solar PPA while also running gas-backed data center capacity is not a contradiction, it is a procurement hedge. Renewable energy certificates and PPA megawatt-hours do not always align with the dispatch profile an always-on compute facility actually requires, and gas is how the gap gets filled. The scrutiny is real, but it will not change the underlying physics: until storage duration extends materially, some portion of hyperscaler load in ERCOT will run on dispatchable thermal generation regardless of what the annual sustainability report says.
