01  The Bottom Line
Pennsylvania's two coal-site redevelopments at Shippingport and Homer City illustrate the spectrum of risk now embedded in AI-scale power strategy: behind-the-meter gas fuel cells versus multi-gigawatt grid-tied generation, and both outcomes hinge on how FERC and PJM resolve co-location standby obligations, a question the Amazon-Talen proceeding at Susquehanna will effectively answer for the industry. Meanwhile, PJM's interconnection queue absorbing 617 projects and roughly 169 GW in Phase 1 confirms that developer appetite is not the bottleneck, but the suspension of the Reliability Backstop Procurement to February 2027 removes a near-term revenue anchor that some of those projects were underwriting financing against. Developers modeling self-supply or large-load interconnection economics across PJM today without a clear standby-charge ruling and a resolved capacity procurement timeline are carrying more unpriced regulatory exposure than their pro formas likely reflect.

Aligned Data Centers, Homer City · 2026-09-30
Data Center Frontier's side-by-side of Aligned's Project Phoenix, which broke ground Sept. 17 at the former Bruce Mansfield site in Shippingport, and the Homer City Energy Campus is the clearest picture yet of Pennsylvania's two paths to AI-scale power. Phoenix leans on on-site gas fuel cells to stay grid-independent; Homer City builds multi-gigawatt central gas generation alongside a proposed AWS campus. Both reuse existing transmission, water, pipeline access and industrial zoning that no greenfield parcel can replicate in three years. The question to track is how each structure fares under PJM's emerging co-location rules, because that will separate durable campus models from ones carrying stranded-cost risk.
02PolicyFERC accepts PJM's Reliability Backstop Procurement proposal but suspends it 5 months to Feb. 28, 2027, delaying Sept. 30 procurement for new…The Read▾
FERC · 2026-09-30
FERC's Sept. 29 order accepts PJM's Reliability Backstop Procurement but suspends it five months, to Feb. 28, 2027, flagging cost allocation, transmission-owner exit rules and LSE collateral as possibly unjust and unreasonable. Chairman Swett called the eleventh-hour filing a "mess." The Sept. 30 offer window is gone and PJM says the new timeline is undetermined, while the 6.8 GW shortfall it was meant to cover is not. Developers who positioned new supply to bid in, under a $555/MW-day offer cap, should revisit financing assumptions tied to a 2026 award and watch PJM's Oct. 7 MIC update.
03Transmission617 projects totaling ~169 GW enter Phase 1 of PJM's new first-ready, first-served interconnection process; demand expected to grow 70 GW by 2038.The Read▾
PJM · 2026-09-30
617 projects and roughly 169 GW of nameplate entering Phase 1 of PJM's first-ready, first-served process is the number that frames everything else in PJM right now. On paper that dwarfs PJM's forecast of up to 70 GW of demand growth by 2038, but nameplate is not delivered capacity: PJM already has 49 GW with signed agreements that is stalled or not being built at all. Gas leads at 81 GW and storage follows at 50 GW, a mix that tells you what developers think will actually clear. The constraint is not developer appetite. It is study capacity, transmission headroom and capital discipline, and positions that already hold signed agreements are the most valuable interconnection assets in the region.
04PolicyWest Virginia's Power Generation and Consumption Act, enabling microgrid-powered data centers outside the grid, faces mounting backlash.The Read▾
West Virginia Power Generation and Consumption Act · 2026-10-01
West Virginia's microgrid data center law was designed to let high-impact facilities build standalone power supplies without touching the grid, and to strip counties of zoning say inside certified microgrid districts. The pushback is coming from the ground up, led by Tucker County residents who want local control back. That matters beyond West Virginia: off-grid exemptions for large load are the template several PJM states are weighing, and the local-control fight is where those models get tested. Developers counting on state preemption to clear siting risk should assume it will be litigated or legislated back.
05PolicyFERC, PJM and state regulators defining treatment of behind-the-meter and co-located data center generation, with Amazon-Talen, Ohio and Michigan as…The Read▾
FERC / PJM / State Regulators · 2026-10-01
The regulatory framing around co-located and self-supplied data centers is converging on a hybrid-grid model, and the Amazon-Talen case at Susquehanna is the one to watch most closely. The core question FERC and PJM are working through is whether grid obligations should be sized to normal net demand or to the full load the system may need to carry during an outage. If regulators land on the larger number, the economics of behind-the-meter generation shift materially because the standby capacity charge becomes the cost of entry, not an afterthought. Ohio and Michigan are developing parallel state-level frameworks that will either reinforce or complicate whatever FERC decides at the federal level. Developers modeling self-supply economics today without a clear answer on standby treatment are underpricing their regulatory exposure.

01TransmissionSenate permitting bill would give FERC authority to permit transmission projects in public interest, eliminate NIETCs and federal right of first…The Read▾
Sens. Lee, Capito, Heinrich & Whitehouse · 2026-10-01
The Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act gives FERC project-by-project transmission permitting authority, eliminates NIETCs and the federal right of first refusal, and assigns transmission costs for 20 MW-plus computational loads to the data centers themselves, with exit charges. It builds on the 2024 Energy Permitting Reform Act, and Whitehouse described talks with the administration as within ordinary legislative disagreement, a signal both sides want to land it. Don't expect action before the midterms: amendments are slated for when the Senate returns in November. If the data center cost-assignment language survives, it changes the economics of every large-load interconnection in PJM and MISO.
