01  The Bottom Line
Nuclear offtake is moving faster than new-build pipelines can absorb: Constellation's Google deal and DOE's conditional loan to Vistra together anchor several gigawatts of existing PJM capacity to long-term contracts and federal credit, shrinking the pool of uncontracted dispatchable megawatts available to developers sourcing power for large loads. Brookfield's entry into the PowerHouse pipeline at nearly 10 GW across nine markets raises a direct question for power originators about whether that procurement closes to outside offtake, while Raleigh's moratorium and Maryland's storage shortfall at 440 MW against an 800 MW target confirm that permitting friction and deliverable capacity constraints are tightening simultaneously across the mid-Atlantic and Southeast. The clearest green light in today's issue is MISO Missouri, where Clearway's $1.5 billion financing close and construction start on 650 MW demonstrate that the region is financeable at scale for developers ready to move.

Constellation Energy · 2026-10-07
Google and Constellation signing a 20-year PPA backing $4.3B of nuclear uprates across 11 PJM reactors is a landmark transaction. Constellation framed the deal as a direct response to PJM's Bring Your Own Power proposal, meaning a hyperscaler just demonstrated the compliance pathway PJM envisioned, and the universe of uncontracted nuclear uprate capacity that could replicate it is finite.

National & Policy  · 3 signals
01Data Centers / IPPBrookfield acquires minority stake in AREP/PowerHouse Data Centers, adding ~10GW development pipeline across 140 US data centers in nine markets.The Read▾
Brookfield, American Real Estate Partners · 2026-10-07
Brookfield acquiring a minority stake in AREP and its PowerHouse Data Centers platform is worth reading carefully. Nearly 10 GW of development pipeline across 140 facilities in nine markets is a land-and-entitlement position at a scale that very few platforms can match. Brookfield already holds Compass and Data4, so this is not a new sector bet; it is vertical integration into the earliest stage of the development funnel, before power is sourced and before interconnection is filed. The question that matters for power originators is whether Brookfield now routes that pipeline's procurement through its own generation and infrastructure capital, which would effectively close a large portion of addressable offtake to outside developers. Watch for interconnection filings in markets where PowerHouse has active campuses.

Raleigh City Council · 2026-10-07
Raleigh's 7-1 vote for a six-month moratorium on data centers above 10,000 SF and 5 MW, driven by community opposition over water, power, and cost concerns, matters because it is the state capital. With Durham, Orange, Chatham, and Harnett counties in similar postures, the North Carolina Piedmont is effectively a constrained development environment. Developers with active site control in the Research Triangle should assume the permitting clock has stopped and plan accordingly.

Alabama Public Service Commission · 2026-10-07
Alabama PSC approved a disclosure framework requiring Alabama Power to name data centers and their peak demand, but without a tariff template it is a transparency mechanism, not a rate structure. The absence of a tariff means the commercial terms for large-load interconnection in Alabama Power territory are still negotiated bilaterally, which is both a risk and an opportunity depending on which side of the table you are on.
