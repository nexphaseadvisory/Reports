01  The Bottom Line
Held for review
PJM is the center of gravity today: a capacity auction that cleared at the price cap for the third straight time, a reliability backstop procurement moving toward FERC, a 1.2GW campus filing in Pennsylvania, and the Nvidia-Ohio mega-campus story all converging in the same constrained footprint. The market is telling developers the same thing the queue has been saying for two years: power in PJM is the scarce asset, not land or capital, and anyone without a contracted position at a real POI is building on spec against an increasingly expensive backstop.

Nebius · 2026-07-22
A 1.2 GW campus on 600 acres in Schuylkill County, Pennsylvania is one of the larger PJM filings in the current cycle, and the detail that matters most is the PPL Electric energy service agreement covering 260 MW live by October 2027. That date is aggressive by any PJM interconnection standard, which means either Nebius holds a queue position that is already well into the study process, or the 260 MW initial phase is served through a distribution-level arrangement that bypasses the transmission queue entirely. The gap between 260 MW operational in 2027 and 1.2 GW total is where the real interconnection risk sits.

03PolicyPJM Board directs FERC filings for Reliability Backstop Procurement and Large Load operational tool amid 70GW load growth forecast and 6.8GW capacity…The Read▾
PJM · 2026-07-27
The PJM board directing FERC filings for both the Reliability Backstop Procurement and a Large Load operational tool in the same motion is the most consequential structural development in this market in years. The backstop auction, which cleared stakeholder vote, would require large loads to directly fund the capacity needed to serve them, shifting cost exposure from ratepayers to developers in a way that reprices every greenfield campus underwriting in the footprint. The Large Load operational tool addresses the real-time reliability gap that the 6.8 GW shortfall creates. Neither is final until FERC approves, but the direction is set and developers who have not modeled the incremental capacity cost obligation need to do that now.

03PolicyTexas PUCT outlines new interconnection rules requiring AI data centers to post financial security and pay direct grid costs, following Gov. Abbott's…The Read▾
Public Utility Commission of Texas · 2026-07-27
Texas is formalizing what the market already suspected: ERCOT is moving to a cost-causation model for large loads, with financial security required before study entry, direct interconnection cost assignment, and transmission charges beginning when capacity is available rather than when the facility operates. That last provision is the one with immediate financial impact, because it means a developer sitting in pre-operational status still accrues transmission cost. Every ERCOT large-load pro forma that assumed a zero-cost pre-operational period needs to be revised.
