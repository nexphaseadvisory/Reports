Tuesday, August 4, 2026
01  The Bottom Line
Two readings from today pull in opposite directions. Exelon's 40% collapse in high-probability data center load after requiring real collateral is the clearest market signal in months: speculative queue pressure is real and transmission security agreements are the new filter. Meanwhile ERCOT's Batch Zero at 205 GW and AEP's 13 GW turbine position tell you that the utilities and grid operators who are actually moving iron have concluded the load wave is structural, not speculative. The gap between those two realities is where site strategy gets decided in 2026.

Data CentersPerimeter Compute launches from stealth to install GPUs in Class A office buildings with 0.5-20MW spare capacity for edge AI inference.The Read▾
Perimeter Compute · 2026-08-03
Office-building spare capacity is a real phenomenon, but Perimeter is solving for the wrong bottleneck. The constraint on AI compute is not half a megawatt in a Manhattan basement; it is hundred-megawatt scale and network latency to hyperscaler backbone. Edge inference has a market, but it is a niche one, and the office-building angle adds lease complexity and cooling risk that a purpose-built facility avoids. Watch whether any of the major cloud providers contract with them rather than build their own edge nodes.

03TransmissionPJM accepts 715 new generation projects totaling over 200 GW into first cycle of reformed interconnection process to address DC-driven demand growth.The Read▾
PJM Interconnection · 2026-08-03
Two hundred gigawatts into the first cycle of PJM's reformed process. The headline number matters less than what it tells you about the composition: the queue is still overwhelmingly aspirational, and the new cluster study design will impose cost allocation in a way that kills a large share of those projects before they reach commercial operation. The developers who survive that process with viable interconnection agreements will hold the most valuable queue positions in North America. Position quality, not queue entry, is the metric to track.

MISO developers pushing back on AI-produced queue studies is a preview of disputes that will end up at FERC.
