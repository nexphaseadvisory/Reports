Tuesday, September 29, 2026
01  The Bottom Line
The offshore wind lease buyback litigation is the most consequential regulatory fight of the summer, and it will not resolve quickly. Meanwhile, rack densities approaching one megawatt per unit are making the power availability question more acute by the quarter. Developers and investors who treat interconnection queue position as a fixed asset rather than a strategy are going to feel it.
02  Signal of the Day

SignalCalifornia and Northeastern AGs sued the Trump administration and offshore wind developers over alleged collusion in lease buyouts, creating…The Read▾
Multiple offshore wind developers (unnamed coalition) · 2026-09-24
Nine state AGs suing both the federal government and developers simultaneously is not a standard environmental challenge. The collusion framing, if it survives a motion to dismiss, turns lease buybacks into potential antitrust exposure rather than a clean policy dispute, which changes how undecided developers calculate whether to take the next deal. Anyone holding interconnection queue positions tied to capacity from projects now in active litigation needs to revisit load forecasts, because that capacity may not arrive on any current schedule.
03  Market Intelligence
National & Policy  · 1 signal
01SignalAI-driven load growth is accelerating demand on utilities, raising grid planning, interconnection, and power-supply challenges for data center and AI…The Read▾
Utilities / Data Center Operators (general) · 2026-09-28
A megawatt per rack is not a planning assumption utilities or ISOs have priced into interconnection study frameworks, and that gap is the real problem. Grid planning cycles built around incremental load additions are already stressed by current AI factory densities, and software tools alone will not solve the timeline problem if per-rack consumption keeps climbing. Site selectors working on AI factory locations should treat transmission capacity and queue position as primary site criteria, not secondary ones.
