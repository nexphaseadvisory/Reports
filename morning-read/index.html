01  The Bottom Line
FERC's rejection of PJM's Interim Resource Adequacy Service removes the proposed mechanism that would have required new large loads to bring their own capacity or face curtailment. energyRe closed an $815 million financing for five South Carolina solar projects totaling about 360 MW, contracted under 25-year power purchase agreements. In Virginia, most state and local officials who spoke at the SCC's Oct. 9 hearing on the Dominion-NextEra merger asked for it to be denied or allowed contingent on guarantees, per Utility Dive; the SCC is set to decide in January.

Our read: A short-term win for data center developers in PJM but one that leaves the resource adequacy gap for the capacity market to resolve. Lenders are still financing contracted solar in the Southeast at scale.
02  Signal of the Day
PJM
PolicyFERC rejected PJM's Interim Resource Adequacy Service proposal that would have required new large loads to bring their own capacity or face curtailment.The Read▾
FERC · per RTO Insider · 2026-10-13
FERC killing the Interim Resource Adequacy Service framework removes the one mechanism PJM had proposed to make new large loads internalize their capacity impact. That is a short-term win for load, but it leaves the resource adequacy gap unsolved.

Our read: FERC's rejection means data center developers in PJM do not face a forced capacity obligation at interconnection under the IRAS framework. Watch for PJM to come back with a revised proposal; the capacity market now bears the burden IRAS would have handled administratively.

01SignalenergyRe secured $815M financing for five South Carolina solar projects totaling 360MW, signaling active development pipeline in the Southeast.The Read▾
energyRe · per PV Tech · 2026-10-13
energyRe closed an $815 million financing package for five utility-scale solar projects in South Carolina totaling about 360 MW, per its release. The five projects are contracted under 25-year power purchase agreements. Bank of America, Canadian Imperial Bank of Commerce and Santander supported the financing. energyRe calls it one of the largest solar-only project financings in South Carolina's history and says it advances a 1.8+ GW portfolio across 20 projects.

Our read: Whether those projects already hold firm transmission access is the open question for anyone underwriting the next round.

Our read: This sets a directional signal for RTO proposals due next month. Developers targeting Duke Carolinas territory now have a cleaner cost signal for what grid access costs under the approved NITSA provisions.
