01  The Bottom Line
FERC's rejection of PJM's IRAS curtailment framework removes the near-term policy threat to large-load developers but leaves PJM without a workable resource adequacy mechanism, and another stakeholder proposal cycle through at least 2027 is the realistic outcome. Meanwhile, PJM's Expedited Interconnection Track is delivering real results, with 2.1 GW from Engie and LS Power fast-tracked to a mid-2029 online date, so developers who can clear the 250 MW threshold with siting authority in hand should be positioning now. The Virginia regulatory climate around the Dominion-NextEra merger adds a separate layer of risk for anyone with pending interconnection requests in Northern Virginia, where a denial or heavily conditioned outcome before the January decision target could reset the ownership structure at the worst possible moment.

04PolicyFERC rejected PJM's Interim Resource Adequacy Service proposal that would have required new large loads to bring their own capacity or face…The Read▾
FERC · per RTO Insider · 2026-10-13
FERC killing the IRAS framework is the most consequential regulatory decision for PJM load development this year. The commission found that requiring large loads to bring their own capacity or face curtailment intrudes on state retail jurisdiction and appears discriminatory. That ruling effectively sends PJM back to the drawing board on large-load resource adequacy, and it reinforces that the federal-state boundary on retail load management is a real constraint, not a procedural footnote. PJM's stated intent to find a path that respects state authority while protecting existing customers is the right framing, but there is no obvious mechanism that satisfies both. Developers with large loads in the PJM queue should expect another proposal cycle and continued uncertainty on the capacity obligation question through at least 2027.
05SignalFERC rejects most of PJM’s large load interconnection planThe Read▾
FERC · per Utility Dive · 2026-10-12
The detail in the FERC order that matters beyond the headline rejection: FERC approved PJM's large-load registry and definition framework, so the data-collection infrastructure survives. The compensation structure and curtailment authority did not. PJM now has to construct a large-load adequacy framework that works without dictating retail terms, and FERC has not told them how. That gap is where the next 18 months of PJM stakeholder process will be spent.
06SignalPJM approves fast-track interconnection for 2.1 GW from Engie, LS PowerThe Read▾
PJM · per Utility Dive · 2026-10-09
PJM fast-tracking 2.1 GW through the Expedited Interconnection Track is the first real proof that the EIT process works. Engie's two storage projects totaling nearly 1.7 GW and LS Power's 455 MW uprate at Hunterstown are all expected online by mid-2029, exactly the window PJM's capacity market has been screaming about. The EIT eligibility bar is high: over 250 MW, primary siting authority in hand, operating within three years. Developers who can clear that bar should be filing now.
