01SignalVirginia regulatory hearing on proposed Dominion-NextEra merger centers on rate impacts and data center power demands under the Virginia Clean…The Read▾
Dominion Energy / NextEra Energy · per Utility Dive · 2026-10-12
The political math on this merger has shifted decisively against NextEra. The lieutenant governor's formal opposition, the governor's stated skepticism, and a parade of elected officials on record at two public hearings all point the same direction heading into a January SCC decision. The dispositive question that the public record has not resolved is what the SCC does with data center cost allocation: the VCEA compliance obligations and ratepayer protection concerns are real, but they are also negotiable with conditions attached. Watch whether the SCC structures a conditional approval around a cost-allocation carve-out rather than an outright denial, because that is the outcome that actually reshapes how Dominion serves hyperscaler load going forward.
02PolicyFERC rejected PJM's Interim Resource Adequacy Service proposal that would have required new large loads to bring their own capacity or face…The Read▾
FERC · per RTO Insider · 2026-10-13
FERC tore out the core of PJM's large-load framework. The IRAS package, the mechanism that would have required new hyperscaler loads to secure matching capacity or face curtailment, is gone. FERC's finding that the framework intrudes on state retail jurisdiction and is potentially discriminatory effectively ends the BYOG mandate in its current form. PJM now has to rebuild a compliance filing around what FERC did approve, which is the large-load definition and registry, and that is a much narrower set of tools than PJM asked for. The practical consequence is that hyperscalers connecting in the near term face fewer self-supply obligations than PJM intended, and the capacity cost socialization question that the IRAS was meant to address gets kicked to state commissions with no uniform answer. This is a favorable outcome for large-load developers in the short run and a problem for everyone on the capacity market side.

04SignalPJM approves fast-track interconnection for 2.1 GW from Engie, LS PowerThe Read▾
PJM · per Utility Dive · 2026-10-09
PJM approved three projects totaling 2.1 GW through its Expedited Interconnection Track: Engie's 860-MW storage project in Crawford, Pennsylvania, an 800-MW storage project in Morrow, Ohio, and an LS Power 455-MW gas-fired uprate, all expected online by mid-2029. This is the first meaningful volume cleared through a purpose-built fast-track process. Whether 2.1 GW materially closes PJM's capacity shortfall remains open, but the pipeline is now moving.

NYISO · per RTO Insider · 2026-10-11
A $216 million system deliverability upgrade tag on a BESS project in New York City is not a surprise, but it is a useful data point on the economics of storage interconnection in constrained load pockets. Projects that cannot absorb that kind of SDU exposure will drop, and the ones that can are either very large or have an offtake that prices in the NYC capacity premium. Elevate's Arthur Kill II position is worth watching to see whether they proceed, resize, or withdraw.

01SignalFERC OKs Duke Energy Carolinas’ Large Load Network Upgrade RulesThe Read▾
FERC · per RTO Insider · 2026-10-13
FERC approving Duke Energy Carolinas' large-load network upgrade cost-allocation rules, while three commissioners simultaneously signaled the industry needs broader reform, is the regulatory pattern to track in vertically integrated territories. Duke moves first, others follow, and each successive filing pushes toward a more explicit cost-causation standard for transmission upgrades triggered by data center load. Developers queuing interconnection in Carolinas Power territory should model the upgrade cost exposure now, because the direction of travel here is toward sharper cost assignment rather than socialization.
