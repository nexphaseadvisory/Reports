01  The Bottom Line
The queue-filtering movement is real and accelerating: utilities are building tariff structures to force capital commitment before they burn study capacity on phantom load, and the DOE's SPARK transmission build is the infrastructure bet that underpins all of it. Meanwhile hyperscalers are locking in nuclear uprate offtake and solar PPAs ahead of the interconnection queue pressure those same loads create, which means the power procurement race is already running faster than the grid can respond.

National & Policy  · 4 signals
01SignalUS utilities and regulators are advancing large-load tariffs and anti-speculation measures to filter uncommitted data center interconnection requests…The Read▾
Multiple utilities and data center developers (sector-wide) · 2026-09-23
This is the most consequential structural shift in interconnection policy since FERC Order 2023. Large-load tariffs that require demonstrated capital commitment before a utility assigns study capacity are exactly the anti-speculation mechanism the queue has needed, and the fact that multiple utilities and state regulators are converging on similar structures in the same window means this becomes a market norm, not an outlier filing. For developers and data center operators, the implication is direct: speculative queue holds as a site-control strategy are getting repriced out of existence. The operators who show up with a signed power agreement and a credible construction schedule will get studied first; everyone else waits longer and pays more. Watch how the tariff definitions treat co-location versus grid-served load, because that boundary will be litigated.
02SignalDOE's Office of Electricity is deploying $5.25B across 31 grid-improvement projects expected to unlock 23GW of additional transmission capacity…The Read▾
US Department of Energy · 2026-09-25
DOE's SPARK program is deploying $5.25 billion across 31 projects to unlock 23 GW of additional transmission capacity via reconductoring and grid-enhancing technology on existing rights-of-way. That is the fastest path to incremental capacity, but it is incremental. The projects sited near targeted corridors capture the benefit first, making corridor-to-queue overlap the actual analytic work.
03SignalGoogle and Georgia Power signed an agreement for Google to support nuclear uprates at two Georgia Power plants to secure additional clean power…The Read▾
Google · 2026-09-21
Google supporting nuclear uprates at Georgia Power's Vogtle and Hatch plants, pending Georgia PSC approval of the NU-1 tariff, is a template worth watching. The structure, a utility-filed tariff monetizing incremental nuclear capacity through zero-emission credits, is precisely the regulatory architecture other state commissions will be asked to replicate as hyperscalers exhaust available clean PPAs. Expect similar filings in states with operating nuclear fleets facing large-load growth pressure.
