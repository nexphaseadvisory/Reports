01  The Bottom Line
PJM's queue landscape shifted materially this week: FERC killed the Interim Resource Adequacy Service framework that would have forced large loads to bring their own generation, removing a constraint that procurement teams had been pricing in, while the Expedited Interconnection Track delivered its first approvals, clearing 2.1 GW from Engie and LS Power for mid-2029 service in a process limited to ten requests per year. In Virginia, the Dominion-NextEra merger is moving toward a January SCC decision with political headwinds and a data center cost-allocation fight that will shape whatever conditions the commission attaches, and Chesterfield County's BZA denial adds another local permitting friction point for site teams working that market. Across PJM and NYISO alike, where a single Arthur Kill battery project is staring at a $216 million deliverability upgrade, the week's common thread is that interconnection and regulatory paths are narrowing at the site level even as federal guardrails on large-load obligations are loosening.

SignalVirginia regulatory hearing on proposed Dominion-NextEra merger centers on rate impacts and data center power demands under the Virginia Clean…The Read▾
Dominion Energy / NextEra Energy · per Utility Dive · 2026-10-12
The Dominion-NextEra merger is now running against a political current that is harder to reverse than a regulatory technicality. Virginia's lieutenant governor has called the deal not in the public interest, the governor is on record as skeptical, and two public hearings have surfaced the same objection each time: data center load growth is being socialized onto residential bills, and the merged entity would have less accountability to state regulators than Dominion does today. The evidentiary hearing opens November 17 and the SCC clock lands in January, so the timeline is fixed. The most likely outcome is not a clean approval. Watch for conditions tied specifically to data center cost allocation methodology, because that is the nerve the public testimony keeps touching, and the SCC will need to address it to survive appellate scrutiny either way.

02PolicyFERC rejected PJM's Interim Resource Adequacy Service proposal that would have required new large loads to bring their own capacity or face…The Read▾
FERC · per RTO Insider · 2026-10-13
FERC rejected PJM's Interim Resource Adequacy Service proposal, which would have required new large loads to bring their own capacity or face curtailment. Why FERC rejected it is not stated in the source, so the practical consequence is what matters: data center developers in PJM no longer face a bring-your-own-generation requirement under this framework, and cost of serving new large loads continues to be socialized across the existing customer base. Procurement teams that built BYOG structures into their PJM strategy should reassess whether that approach still pencils.

04SignalPJM approves fast-track interconnection for 2.1 GW from Engie, LS PowerThe Read▾
PJM · per Utility Dive · 2026-10-09
PJM's Expedited Interconnection Track clearing 2.1 GW in its first application window, with Engie's 1.7 GW of storage and LS Power's 455 MW gas uprate both targeting mid-2029 service, is real capacity movement in a market where the queue has been nearly frozen for years. The process is capped at 10 requests per year and requires a primary siting authority backing, so it is not a broad solution, but it is a live track for developers who can meet the threshold. For anyone holding a conventional queue position in PJM planning a 2029 in-service date, this is competition for the same capacity market window. The LS Power gas uprate at Hunterstown also matters because it adds firm dispatchable capacity at an existing interconnection point, which is faster than a new POI by a wide margin.

FERC accepted Duke Energy Carolinas' tariff changes to reduce cost-shift risk from large load network upgrades, and three commissioners stated that broader reforms are still needed. Developers taking service from IOUs in the Southeast should treat this approval as a floor, not a ceiling, on what cost-allocation rules will look like in two years.
