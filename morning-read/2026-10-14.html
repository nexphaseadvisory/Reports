01  The Bottom Line
Capital is moving across multiple markets simultaneously: energyRe closing $815M for 360MW of South Carolina solar, Foss and Company completing a $58M tax equity flip for GridStor's Texas BESS, and Clearway expanding its Utah storage fleet to 2,120MWh all confirm that financing execution is not the bottleneck right now. The constraint is regulatory clarity in PJM, where FERC's rejection of the Interim Resource Adequacy Service framework on state-jurisdiction and discrimination grounds leaves large-load developers without a timeline anchor for capacity supply decisions. PJM must now redesign from the approved registry framework up, and that process will consume months while the interconnection queue continues to grow.

02PolicyFERC rejected PJM's Interim Resource Adequacy Service proposal that would have required new large loads to bring their own capacity or face…The Read▾
FERC · per RTO Insider · 2026-10-13
FERC rejecting PJM's IRAS framework is the most consequential regulatory outcome in PJM load policy this year. The commission found the bring-your-own-capacity curtailment mechanism intrudes on state retail jurisdiction and is potentially discriminatory. PJM now has to rebuild from the approved registry framework up. The state-federal jurisdictional line FERC drew here will constrain whatever PJM proposes next, and the large-load queue is not getting shorter while that redesign happens. Data center developers who were banking on IRAS clarity before making capacity supply decisions just lost their timeline anchor.
