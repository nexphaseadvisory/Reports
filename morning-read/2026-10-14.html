01  The Bottom Line
PJM's IRAS rejection removes the near-term curtailment threat for large loads but leaves capacity cost exposure unresolved and pushes the next fight into state commissions, particularly Virginia, Maryland, and Ohio. In Virginia, the Dominion-NextEra evidentiary hearing on November 17 will determine what conditions the SCC is actually willing to accept, and the outcome shapes Dominion's entire capital and grid investment plan at a moment when data center cost allocation is still unsettled. Developers moving capacity in PJM should also note that the Expedited Interconnection Track just cleared 2.1 GW for Engie and LS Power targeting mid-2029, making that fast-track lane the most actionable near-term option for site-controlled projects while the broader framework gets rebuilt.

SignalVirginia regulatory hearing on proposed Dominion-NextEra merger centers on rate impacts and data center power demands under the Virginia Clean…The Read▾
Dominion Energy / NextEra Energy · per Utility Dive · 2026-10-12
The political math in Virginia is hardening against this deal faster than the regulatory clock will resolve it. The lieutenant governor has called it against the public interest, the governor is formally a respondent and self-described skeptic, and the public hearing pattern is running lopsided against approval. The evidentiary hearing starts November 17 and SCC decision comes in January, so there is not much runway for sentiment to reverse. The central unresolved question at the commission level is data center cost allocation: who pays for the grid upgrades that Northern Virginia's hyperscale load requires, and what the VCEA compliance trajectory looks like under NextEra ownership. If the merger is denied or conditioned in ways NextEra finds uneconomical, Dominion's capital plan and its grid investment schedule both become open questions. Watch the November evidentiary record for what conditions SCC staff are actually willing to bless, because that is where the real deal terms get written.

02PolicyFERC rejected PJM's Interim Resource Adequacy Service proposal that would have required new large loads to bring their own capacity or face…The Read▾
FERC · per RTO Insider · 2026-10-13
FERC's rejection of IRAS is the most consequential regulatory outcome in the PJM large-load space in months. The commission found that requiring new large loads to procure matching capacity or face curtailment crosses into retail regulation and state jurisdiction, which means PJM has no enforceable backstop mechanism for the load additions currently stressing its resource adequacy position. The capacity cost spiral continues with no structural check on the demand side. PJM now has to rebuild a proposal that avoids retail rate-setting, which is a narrow lane given the underlying logic of the framework. For developers and data center operators in PJM: the near-term path to interconnection just got less encumbered, but the medium-term capacity price exposure got worse, and the political pressure on states to act unilaterally is now much higher.

This is the same FERC IRAS rejection covered above. The Utility Dive account adds one detail worth keeping: PJM explicitly said it will look for ways to honor FERC's requirements while protecting existing customers from cost and reliability burdens. That is a narrower mandate than the original IRAS design and suggests the next filing will try to push cost internalization onto states rather than through tariff mechanisms. Watch the state commission responses in Virginia, Maryland, and Ohio, because that is where this lands next.
04SignalPJM approves fast-track interconnection for 2.1 GW from Engie, LS PowerThe Read▾
PJM · per Utility Dive · 2026-10-09
PJM's Expedited Interconnection Track has approved 2.1 GW in its first cohort: Engie's two storage projects totaling nearly 1.7 GW and LS Power's 455 MW gas-fired uprate, both targeting mid-2029 commercial operation. The fast-track process is designed for large, site-controlled projects that can clear studies on a compressed timeline. For developers with credible near-term paths to operations, this is the most actionable interconnection lane in PJM right now.
