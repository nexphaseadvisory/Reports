01  The Bottom Line
Vistra's conditional $4.2 billion DOE loan locking nearly 4 GW of PJM nuclear toward mid-century and Amazon's 20-year PPA at Calvert Cliffs together compress the long-dated baseload available to other buyers in the Mid-Atlantic zone, so any developer still structuring PJM offtake without a firm resource answer should treat that window as closing. On the siting front, local veto risk is rising in parallel across every footprint: Pennsylvania's Environmental Rights Amendment is becoming a standard litigation tool, Tonganoxie's organized opposition against Project Bluestem mirrors the 2017 playbook that killed the last major proposal there, and Texas is pausing certain data center approvals just as ERCOT carries the heaviest load queue volume, making already-permitted sites with queue positions materially more valuable. The SPS Tolk retirement replacing 1,700-plus MW of coal with 3,700-plus MW of new generation in 2029 is the single largest resource adequacy reshaping event in SPP right now, and developers with generation or load in that footprint should be modeling post-retirement congestion before the replacement capacity saturates the local zone.

A conditional $4.2B DOE loan to uprate Beaver Valley, Davis-Besse, and Perry adds 433MW of incremental capacity, but the more consequential fact is the implied 20-year life extension: nearly 4GW of baseload in PJM that could have exited the market is now likely to run toward mid-century. 05Data Centers / IPPAmazon and Constellation sign 20-year PPA to expand 1,790-MW Calvert Cliffs nuclear plant in MarylandThe Read▾
Constellation Energy · 2026-10-05
Amazon signing a 20-year PPA for 690MW from Calvert Cliffs, including the 190MW uprate, is the kind of deal that reprices every nuclear-adjacent power position in PJM. A 20-year commitment from AWS to a specific Maryland nuclear plant is not a hedge, it is a declaration that Amazon views firm, carbon-free baseload in PJM as scarce enough to lock in for two decades at whatever price clears. The retail supply agreement covering Amazon's broader PJM operations layered on top of the direct PPA is the structural tell: this is a whole-portfolio power strategy, not a project-level sustainability box-check. The question the source does not answer is whether the uprate requires a separate interconnection filing or falls under Calvert Cliffs' existing service agreement, because 190MW of incremental nuclear capacity in that zone will draw attention from every party watching PJM's capacity margin. Anyone else holding a long-dated power position in PJM's Mid-Atlantic zone should be benchmarking their contract structure against this deal.

MISO · 2026-10-05
MISO's LARS proposal is the most operationally significant queue reform the ISO has put forward for large loads. The 120-day fast-track study process is available for loads over 200MW paired with new generation in the same local resource zone, and a commercial operation deadline filters out speculative filings. For a developer with a real load commitment and a generation asset in the same local resource zone, this is the fastest available path to MISO interconnection.

01PolicyCAISO and PG&E clash over proposed 'large load' definition as AI data centers press California's grid for interconnection.The Read▾
CAISO, Pacific Gas and Electric · 2026-10-04
CAISO and PG&E disagreeing on the large-load definition ahead of a board vote is not procedural noise. The definition determines which projects face enhanced study requirements, cost allocation, and potentially curtailment obligations. If PG&E wins a narrower definition, more projects slip under the threshold and avoid enhanced scrutiny. If CAISO prevails with a broader definition, California becomes materially harder for large-load developers to navigate and some projects will re-evaluate Nevada or Arizona alternatives. The board vote is the inflection point to track.
