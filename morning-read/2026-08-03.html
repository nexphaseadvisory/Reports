01  The Bottom Line
Held for review
PJM is structurally broken and knows it: three consecutive capped auctions, a 15 GW generation deficit, and now a board-approved framework that would make 70 GW of incoming AI load self-provision capacity or accept curtailment. That is not a market refinement, it is a load-growth tax, and every data center developer with an unsigned PPA in PJM should be repricing their power risk right now. The rest of the signals confirm the same thesis: central Ohio is building its own grid, ERCOT is absorbing demand that cannot wait for reform, and the hyperscalers are spending at a pace that assumes the power will arrive on schedule, which is the one assumption least supported by the evidence.
Editorial thesis, held pending review before send.

TransmissionPJM begins accepting Expedited Interconnection Track applications for large generation projects ≥250 MW UCAP that can commence within 3 yearsThe Read▾
PJM · 2026-07-31
PJM opening an Expedited Interconnection Track for projects at or above 250 MW UCAP that can reach commercial operation within three years is aimed squarely at the supply shortfall its last three capacity auctions have exposed. The hard question is how many projects actually meet the three-year commercial operation test. Most gas projects in PJM that are not already in late-stage development cannot make that window. BESS projects can, and that makes this track more relevant to storage developers and peakers than to new combined cycle.

03PolicyPJM filed FERC proposal for Reliability Backstop Procurement launching Sept 2026 to secure new supply against 6,831 MW shortfall at up to $555/MW-day…The Read▾
PJM · 2026-07-31
The Reliability Backstop Procurement proposal matters more than its Actionability score implies. A mechanism offering up to $555/MW-day on 15-year terms is designed to pull forward supply that the existing RPM auction, capped at $325/MW-day, cannot attract. If FERC approves this, developers with gas or storage projects that can clear the reliability screen will face a very different contracting conversation than they do today. The 6,831 MW shortfall that triggered the filing is the number to hold: that is the gap PJM is admitting it cannot fill through normal market mechanics.
04PolicyPJM to file FERC framework requiring new large loads (AI data centers) to bring ~70GW of capacity or accept curtailments amid 15GW of retirements.Held for reviewThe Read▾
PJM Interconnection · 2026-07-31
PJM's board-approved reliability framework is the most consequential load policy any U.S. grid operator has proposed. The mechanics are straightforward: bring capacity equal to your demand, or accept curtailment when PJM runs short. Against a backdrop of 70 GW of projected large-load additions through 2038, the math is not subtle. For developers with sites in PJM who have not yet signed power supply arrangements, this framework converts what was a market risk into a potential service condition. FERC will push back on some details, but the directional outcome is clear: colocation and campus power self-supply are becoming requirements.
