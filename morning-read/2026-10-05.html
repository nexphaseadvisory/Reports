01  The Bottom Line
The Nscale-Anthropic commitment in Mason County and Crusoe's Jayton filing confirm that nine-figure capital is moving into sites where power contracting is still unresolved, which means interconnection queues and cost allocation fights will define project timelines more than construction schedules. Pennsylvania PUC's curtailment ruling, MISO's reserve margin methodology shift toward forecasting, and the Senate bill's proposed requirement that data center operators fund associated transmission costs are three separate levers converging on the same pressure point: large-load developers are losing the assumption that grid costs and reliability priority will be shared broadly. Amazon's lock on Generac capacity through 2028 adds a fourth constraint, and any developer without backup generation under contract for that window should treat procurement as a critical-path item today.

03SignalMISO Tries Different Tack for PRMR Allocation, Decides on Forecasting ApproachThe Read▾
MISO · 2026-10-01
MISO abandoning its settled-load approach to planning reserve margin requirement allocation in favor of a forecasting methodology is a consequential change for load-serving entities carrying large-load growth. Forecasts in a period of 20% CAGR large-load growth will consistently run ahead of settled actuals, which means LSE reserve obligations will be set higher and earlier. Anyone underwriting capacity obligations for MISO LSEs with significant data center exposure needs to revisit the reserve margin math.
