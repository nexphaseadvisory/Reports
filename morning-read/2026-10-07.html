01  The Bottom Line
Capital is moving faster than interconnection queues in every major ISO, and this issue makes that gap concrete: Enerflex's 450MW behind-the-meter gas deal and Fit Energy's fuel cell data center in Luzerne County are both symptoms of developers accepting premium fuel costs to avoid queue exposure, not isolated experiments. Maryland's conditional 440MW BESS award and Virginia's Spanberger framework show states actively trying to redirect that load growth pressure into structured procurement and clean generation obligations, but the ISO-NE cluster complaint and the MISO-SPP-PJM freeze-date renegotiation are reminders that the transmission infrastructure those frameworks depend on is still contested at the seam level. With US Bank flagging capital expenditure pressure from rising rates and $1.3B in fresh PV financing still closing, developers need to stress-test project economics against a sustained higher-rate environment before the next round of interconnection bids locks in cost assumptions.

Anne Arundel County
Anne Arundel County approved a moratorium on new data center development through 2028, citing community concerns. The county sits in one of PJM's most congested load pockets, so halting permitting in a supply-constrained zone does not reduce load growth; it redirects it to adjacent counties that may have even less transmission headroom. Developers who already hold zoning elsewhere in central Maryland should treat this as a competitive advantage.

ISO-NE · 2026-10-05
A FERC complaint against ISO-NE's first Order 2023 cluster study is not surprising, but the framing matters. Developers are calling the cost outputs project-killing, which means the methodology dispute is about whether projects clear or die, not just about allocation percentages. ISO-NE's cluster process was already the most opaque in the Northeast, and this complaint forces FERC to either validate or correct the study structure before the next cycle runs. Anyone with a position in ISO-NE's queue should be tracking this proceeding, because a FERC ruling that requires restudying will reset timelines for the entire cohort.

MISO · 2026-10-05
MISO, SPP, and PJM updating the 2004 freeze date for firm flow entitlements is a transmission seam issue that has been unresolved for years. Getting all three RTOs to agree on a replacement methodology is harder than it sounds, and the prior attempts failed. This matters most for projects near the MISO-PJM and MISO-SPP seams where M2M settlement drives curtailment exposure. Monitor the FERC filing when it comes, because the new date will reprice transmission rights on seam projects.
