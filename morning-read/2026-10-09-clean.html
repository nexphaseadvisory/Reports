01  The Bottom Line
PJM's Expedited Interconnection Track has its first three projects, DOE is pressing FERC to sharpen cost accountability for large loads, and Mon Power's $145 million Fern complaint adds another vector of pressure on how network and dispatch costs get assigned across the footprint. Developers moving toward PJM commercial operation should lock down load commitments and offtake before cost-allocation rules harden, as the Google-Constellation nuclear PPA and Voltus's $225 million Series D both reflect a market accelerating toward firm, dispatchable products that sidestep queue and allocation exposure. LS Power's roughly $6 billion Fund VI closing oversubscribed means capital competition for generation assets is at a cycle high, and anyone still sitting in MISO's 2022 queue needs to watch upgrade cost reallocation mechanics before deciding whether the one-time exit option works in their favor.

PJM · 2026-10-08
PJM accepting the first three projects under its Expedited Interconnection Track, two batteries and a gas uprate totaling more than 2,100 MW targeting mid-2029 service, is proof of concept for the mechanism but not a solution to the queue backlog. Three projects is a thin inaugural class for a program designed to address a multi-gigawatt capacity shortfall. The more telling number is what the study process cost each of these developers in time and dollars versus a standard cluster position; if the expedited track delivers 2029 commercial operation without the usual restudy hell, it will attract a longer queue for the next round. Track that application window.

03PolicyDOE files at FERC urging PJM to protect ratepayers from large-load (data center) cost allocation by tracking project status and assigning costs…The Read▾
DOE · 2026-10-08
DOE filing at FERC to push PJM toward continuous project tracking and cost assignment is a rare direct intervention, and the timing is not accidental. It arrives while the backstop procurement order is on hold after FERC flagged cost allocation as potentially unjust, and while PJM is racing to file a revised proposal by October 29. The practical implication for large-load developers is that the regulatory trajectory in PJM is toward sharper cost accountability: if your project triggers network upgrades and you do not reach commercial operation, the pressure to assign those costs to you rather than to the broader rate base is building from multiple directions simultaneously. Get load commitments papered before the cost-allocation rules harden.
04Data Centers / IPPGoogle PPA backs 890 MW of nuclear uprates at Constellation plants to serve PJM data center load.The Read▾
Google News
Google signing a PPA backing 890 MW of nuclear uprates at Constellation plants to serve PJM data center load is the procurement model every large-load developer should be studying. Uprates at operating plants clear faster than new builds, avoid the interconnection queue entirely for the incremental capacity, and hand the buyer a firm, around-the-clock product. This is the second major Google nuclear procurement in PJM inside twelve months, and the competitive consequence is that the path to contracted nuclear for the next hyperscaler is narrowing.
05SignalMon Power Complains PJM’s Conservative Operations During Fern Cost it $145MThe Read▾
Monongahela Power · 2026-10-07
Mon Power's $145 million complaint against PJM over Winter Storm Fern dispatch decisions is a significant cost-allocation fight that will reshape how PJM documents conservative operations going forward. The core dispute is whether advance scheduling and commitment of gas generation during extreme cold was operationally justified or excessive, a question FERC will have to resolve on the merits. For developers and load-serving entities, the outcome sets a precedent on how storm-event costs are distributed across the PJM footprint.

Clearway Energy Group · 2026-10-07
A $1.5 billion construction start on 650 MW in Bates County with a 20-year PPA already in hand is a textbook close: financing syndicated, offtake locked, shovel in the ground. Clearway's first Missouri project is also part of a larger 1.2-GW portfolio they announced earlier this year, so the pipeline behind this is real and the MISO queue pressure it represents extends well beyond this single site. 02TransmissionMISO's FERC proposal for one-time 2022 queue exit option and restudy cap drew mixed reactions over upgrade cost reassignment.The Read▾
MISO · 2026-10-07
MISO's one-time exit option for the 2022 queue cycle is a pragmatic mechanism to clear speculative positions that are clogging restudies, but cost reassignment is where it gets contentious. If a developer exits and their allocated upgrade costs shift to remaining projects, the viability calculation for those projects changes overnight. For anyone still sitting in the 2022 cycle, the strategic question is not whether to use the exit option but whether to wait and see how upgrade cost reallocation shakes out before deciding. The FERC proceeding on this is the one to watch.

NextEra suspending two Long Island BESS facilities over drinking water contamination is a siting and community-trust problem, not an isolated equipment failure.
