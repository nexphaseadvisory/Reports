01  The Bottom Line
The policy and cost-assignment moves are converging: Abbott's ERCOT queue audit, the Virginia SCC transmission cost order, Heinrich's GRID Savings Act, and PJM's reliability backstop procurement all point the same direction. Large loads will increasingly pay directly for the grid they consume, and developers who cannot demonstrate real projects with real principals are about to find that out the hard way. Origis marketing a fresh 1 GW ERCOT co-location opportunity and Avantus closing $1.05B on a 24 GW pipeline show the supply side is moving to meet that demand, but the interconnection and cost-allocation screws are tightening simultaneously.
02  Signal of the Day
ERCOT
PolicyTexas Gov. Abbott orders PUCT/ERCOT audit of 474GW large-load queue (90% data centers), threatening to deny grid access for non-compliant projects.The Read▾
Public Utility Commission of Texas, Electric Reliability Council of Texas · 2026-08-04
Governor Abbott ordering a comprehensive audit of ERCOT's 474 GW large-load queue, with the explicit threat of denying grid access for non-compliant projects, is the most consequential near-term event in ERCOT interconnection. The queue at five times Texas peak demand was always going to draw regulatory action; the question was who moved first and how hard. Abbott moved hard, tying compliance to existing state disclosure law on water and power usage, which gives the PUCT a legal hook that is harder to challenge than a pure policy preference. Developers who filed queue positions without completing the PUCT survey are now staring at a real denial risk, not a delay. The practical outcome is a forced culling of speculative positions, which is what the queue has needed, but the mechanism is blunt and the timeline for the audit is not specified. Any project that cannot document ownership, financing, and community impact fully should treat this as a hard stop and get compliant immediately.

01PolicyVirginia SCC orders changes to Dominion's transmission cost allocation, cutting residential ratepayer impact by two-thirds and teeing up direct cost…The Read▾
Virginia State Corporation Commission · 2026-08-04
The Virginia SCC cutting residential ratepayers' share of Dominion transmission costs by two-thirds and teeing up direct cost assignment to large loads for future proceedings is a foundational shift in how Virginia will allocate infrastructure costs going forward. It is also a signal that the largest existing data center market in the country is moving toward a cost structure that makes new entrants more expensive and existing large loads more exposed to retroactive allocation proceedings. The SCC explicitly left direct cost assignment for future proceedings, which means the full reckoning has not arrived yet. Developers underwriting Virginia sites on current cost assumptions should treat that docket as a live risk.

The FCC inverter restriction is a genuine supply-chain disruption for solar and BESS developers, not a background compliance item.
