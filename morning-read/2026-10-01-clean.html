01  The Bottom Line
New York's storage procurement and SPP West's resource adequacy fight are the two market-structure stories that matter this week. The NYSERDA awards confirm the 6 GW storage build is real and moving, which reprices every co-located interconnection position in NYISO. SPP West is the more consequential regulatory risk: a stop-gap RA mechanism that the grid's own market monitor calls gameable and anticompetitive is heading toward a FERC decision that will set the rules for a still-forming Western market. Position accordingly.

Labor and equipment shortages, combined with local opposition, are threatening high-profile data center projects backed by major tech companies. New anti-stockpiling rules are designed to prevent buyers from front-running the December Section 232 tariff date on polysilicon imports. Solar developers who have not locked supply are now exposed to both price pressure and constrained availability simultaneously. Anyone underwriting a utility-scale solar project on pre-tariff module cost assumptions needs to reopen that model today.
