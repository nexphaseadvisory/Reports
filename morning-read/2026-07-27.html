01  The Bottom Line
Held for review
The week's signals point to a market under structural stress at every layer: PJM capacity is broken enough that FERC is threatening to take the wheel by September, the Ashburn fault event proved that 3 GW of hyperscale load can disappear from the grid in seconds, and Congress is moving bipartisan legislation to make large loads pay their own network upgrade costs. Developers chasing power in established corridors face regulatory blowback, community opposition, and a queue that cannot clear fast enough, which means the edge plays in ERCOT and the brownfield retrofit model are getting a second look for good reason.

02  Signal of the Day
PJM
SignalFERC will impose reforms if PJM fails to adopt changes by September, chairman warnsThe Read▾
FERC · 2026-07-24
FERC giving PJM until the end of September to agree to governance and board independence reforms, with a credible threat to impose them unilaterally, is the kind of regulatory pressure that changes how stakeholders behave in the near term. Chairman Swett's framing of a "grave legitimacy crisis" and transmission owners openly discussing leaving the RTO is not routine conference language. The immediate market implication is that any PJM filing, whether on the reliability backstop, interconnection reform, or capacity market rules, now sits under a September deadline that concentrates political will in ways that slow-moving stakeholder processes normally do not. Developers with positions in front of the PJM board or FERC in the next 60 days should treat the governance pressure as a tailwind for reform, not as noise.

Trammell Crow and Hanover calling Lehigh Valley a non-starter for data center development because of site scarcity, inflated land prices, and entitlement opposition is a useful data point for anyone still running that play. 03TransmissionTransmission fault in Ashburn, VA triggered 3 GW of data center load to transfer to backup power on PJM grid.Held for reviewThe Read▾
Dominion Energy · 2026-07-23
Three gigawatts of data center load dropping from the PJM grid in seconds after a single transmission fault in Ashburn is the reliability stress test that grid planners had not yet seen in the field at this scale. Dominion's statement that the data centers' own protection systems initiated the transfer, and that no load was actually shed, is technically correct but misses the point. A sudden demand swing of that magnitude from a single geographic cluster is a planning event, not a routine switching operation. Anyone sizing a campus in Northern Virginia today needs to understand that the density of load in that corridor creates correlated risk that individual facility resiliency plans do not address.

02SignalMISO to Ask for FERC Approval to Pass over Late Affected Studies in QueueThe Read▾
MISO · 2026-07-24
MISO seeking FERC permission to proceed with 2022 and 2023 queue cycle studies even when neighboring ISOs have not completed their affected systems studies is a meaningful procedural move. Affected systems delays have been one of the primary reasons interconnection timelines slip past their study milestones, and the MISO-PJM seam is among the worst offenders. If FERC approves this, it establishes a precedent that an ISO can decouple its study progress from a neighbor's pace. Developers with projects in those MISO cycles who have been waiting on PJM affected systems results should watch this filing closely.

01SignalPanamint Capital broke ground on a 1.2GWdc solar PV project at Twin Oaks power station in Robertson County, Texas.Held for reviewThe Read▾
Panamint Capital · 2026-07-24
The brownfield angle is real but do not let the press release obscure the execution risk. Panamint is building 1.2 GWdc of solar on an active coal mining site in Robertson County with its own 345kV backbone, which means the transmission story is self-funded rather than queued through ERCOT. That structure, a developer controlling the point of interconnection and owning the land, buys a fundamentally different interconnection timeline than anyone waiting in the standard large-load process. Phase one at 491 MWdc targets August 2028 commercial operation. If that schedule holds, this becomes a reference project for the integrated campus model in Texas.

04PolicyTexas PUC proposes legislative remedies to give it and ERCOT more authority over large-load (data center) interconnections as peak demand hits…The Read▾
Public Utility Commission of Texas · 2026-07-24
Texas PUC asking the legislature for more authority over large-load interconnections while ERCOT sets new system peak demand records is the regulatory signal that the current self-certification model for large loads in Texas is approaching its political limit. The PUC does not currently have the tools to sequence, condition, or delay large-load interconnection requests the way PJM does, and peak demand records create the political urgency to change that. Developers with ERCOT large-load interconnection requests in process should expect that the legislative session produces new procedural requirements, and the projects that are furthest along in study have the most to gain from getting there before any new rules take effect.

03SignalCongress advanced the Ratepayer Protection Act 52-0 while the White House expanded pledges to prevent data centers from shifting grid upgrade costs…Held for reviewThe Read▾
Data center developers / hyperscalers (sector-wide) · 2026-07-23
A 52-0 committee vote on the Ratepayer Protection Act is not a close call. The bill would direct state commissions to consider requiring 100 MW and larger loads to cover full network upgrade costs. That is the cost-causation principle that most state commissions already apply in theory but rarely enforce at scale. If this clears Congress, it hardens soft policy into a statutory floor and will directly affect how hyperscalers negotiate utility service agreements for large campuses. Developers in states where this legislation has traction should assume the incremental transmission cost is their cost, not the ratepayer's, and model accordingly.
