Bottom Line
Federal capital deployment and tightening interconnection screens are reshaping the U.S. power landscape simultaneously from the top down and the bottom up. The DOE's $5.25B commitment to 23GW of new transmission capacity is opening fresh queue corridors, while utilities and regulators are closing speculative entry points through large-load tariffs and anti-speculation measures. Data center load growth continues to pressure both interconnection queues and clean power supply, driving hyperscalers toward unconventional procurement structures including nuclear uprates. Active IPPs such as Apex Clean Energy are closing PPAs in competitive markets, signaling that well-positioned developers with credible interconnection strategies retain a meaningful edge. The convergence of federal infrastructure investment, tightening queue screens, and surging firm-power demand marks a pivotal inflection point for interconnection advisory and power origination.

US Department of Energy
The DOE's Office of Electricity announced $5.25B in federal funding directed at 31 grid-improvement projects expected to unlock 23GW of additional transmission capacity across the United States.
Airtable  Open record
Read  This commitment represents one of the largest single federal outlays for transmission infrastructure in recent history, signaling durable, policy-backed momentum for new corridor development over the next several years. Developers and IPPs with active or planned interconnection applications should map their pipelines against the 31 funded projects immediately, as newly unlocked capacity windows will be competitive and time-sensitive.
Strategic Thought  Federal transmission investment at this scale directly expands the addressable queue landscape for interconnection advisors, creating new entry points in corridors that were previously constrained or uneconomic. Clients holding near-term-ready projects in affected regions gain a first-mover advantage if queue applications are filed ahead of speculative entrants who will race to capture newly available capacity. Advisors with detailed project-level mapping against DOE corridor awards will be positioned to deliver high-value strategic guidance that generalist consultants cannot replicate quickly.
2
Multiple utilities and data center developers (sector-wide)
Utilities and state regulators across the U.S. are advancing large-load interconnection tariffs and anti-speculation deposit requirements designed to filter uncommitted or financially unserious data center interconnection requests from the queue.
Airtable  Open record
Read  The regulatory shift effectively raises the bar for queue entry, disadvantaging applicants who lack demonstrated site control, financial credibility, or load certainty at the time of application. Developers and data center buyers who fail to adapt their queue strategies to these new screening criteria risk rejection, withdrawal, or loss of queue position to better-credentialed competitors.
Strategic Thought  Tightening interconnection screens create a structural premium for advisory expertise in queue positioning, deposit structuring, and project credentialing—skills that were less differentiated when entry barriers were lower. The sector-wide nature of this trend means demand for specialized interconnection advisory services is likely to broaden geographically rather than concentrate in a single market. As utilities codify anti-speculation measures into tariff language, the cost of filing without expert preparation rises materially, expanding the value proposition for pre-application advisory engagements.

Google  GEORGIA
Google and Georgia Power reached an agreement under which Google will financially support nuclear uprates at two existing Georgia Power plants in exchange for access to additional clean firm power capacity.
Airtable  Open record
Read  The structure represents a notable departure from conventional corporate PPA procurement, with a hyperscaler directly co-investing in existing generation asset enhancement to secure long-duration, carbon-free baseload power. This signals that large data center operators in constrained markets are willing to accept more complex, capital-intensive procurement arrangements when conventional renewable supply is insufficient to meet firm-power requirements.
Strategic Thought  The emergence of utility nuclear uprate agreements as a corporate clean-power procurement mechanism reflects a deepening mismatch between hyperscaler load growth and available dispatchable renewable supply in regulated markets. For the interconnection and power origination advisory sector, this trend suggests that advisory mandates will increasingly require multi-modal procurement expertise spanning PPAs, utility commercial agreements, and non-conventional clean energy structures. Advisors who can help clients evaluate and sequence conventional interconnection pathways alongside complementary firm-power strategies will be better positioned as data center buyers exhaust standard renewable supply options in key regions.
