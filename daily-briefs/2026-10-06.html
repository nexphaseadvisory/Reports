Bottom Line
The U.S. power and data infrastructure market is accelerating on multiple fronts simultaneously, with large-scale coal retirement creating gigawatt-class procurement voids, behind-the-meter generation emerging as a structural requirement for hyperscale data center development in constrained grid environments, and federal capital flows into nuclear uprates reshaping PJM capacity injection and PPA structuring dynamics. Texas remains the most active single battleground, where ERCOT's grid-connection pause is pushing developers toward on-site BTM generation solutions at the same time SPP faces a 3.7 GW replacement mandate in the same regional footprint. Internationally, brownfield former power station redevelopments are attracting data center capital, reinforcing that interconnection complexity — not land — is the binding constraint on development timelines. The convergence of coal retirements, nuclear uprates, and hyperscale power demand is compressing the window in which originators and interconnection advisors can add maximum value before developers lock counterparties. Market signals today collectively favor advisors with multi-ISO queue strategy depth and structured PPA execution capability.

Airtable  Open record
Read  A 3.7 GW replacement mandate in SPP's Texas-New Mexico footprint represents one of the largest single utility procurement cycles currently active in the region, with a hard 2029 deadline creating urgent developer and IPP engagement timelines. This is a capital-attracting event that will draw independent power producers, renewable developers, and storage originators into SPP queue positions in the near term.
Strategic Thought  Coal plant retirement of this scale generates a concentrated wave of new interconnection applications in the SPP queue, increasing study cycle congestion and elevating the value of queue positioning strategy and cluster study navigation expertise. PPA origination advisors with SPP-specific counterparty relationships are positioned to structure offtake agreements for the developers competing to fill this procurement void. The 2029 retirement timeline back-solves to interconnection application windows opening now, making the current 12-to-18-month period the highest-leverage entry point for advisory engagement in this cycle.

PowerHouse Data Centers, Big Watt Digital  TEXAS
PowerHouse Data Centers and Big Watt Digital are seeking annexation and zoning approval from Taylor, Texas, for a 664-acre campus targeting 500 MW of capacity expandable to 1 GW, incorporating behind-the-meter on-site generation.
Airtable  Open record
Read  The explicit inclusion of BTM generation in the project structure signals that the developer is designing around ERCOT's grid-connection pause rather than waiting for queue clearance, a pattern that is becoming standard for large Texas hyperscale campuses. A 500 MW to 1 GW BTM build-out requires sophisticated on-site generation contracting, fuel sourcing, and interconnection boundary structuring that differs materially from traditional grid-tied PPA execution.
Strategic Thought  The BTM generation model at this scale effectively creates a mini-utility on-site, requiring interconnection agreements at the campus boundary, standby service negotiations with the local distribution provider, and structured agreements for supplemental grid capacity — all distinct advisory workstreams from a standard queue filing. As ERCOT's pause continues, the BTM-first architecture is likely to become the default for new large-load Texas developments, shifting advisory demand toward hybrid on-site and grid-coordination expertise. This signal confirms that power origination and interconnection advisory value in ERCOT is migrating from transmission-level queue strategy toward the BTM structuring and distributed generation contracting layer.

While this specific project is outside U.S. ISO/RTO jurisdictions, the structural dynamics it illustrates — BTM complexity at brownfield power sites — are directly analogous to U.S. coal and nuclear retirement site redevelopment opportunities emerging in PJM, MISO, and SPP.
