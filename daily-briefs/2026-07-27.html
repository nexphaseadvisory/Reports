Bottom Line
The data center power market is entering a structural inflection point where developers can no longer assume grid capacity will be allocated to them passively — ISO-NE's proposed bring-your-own-generation mandate is the clearest regulatory signal yet that large loads must arrive at the queue with generation solutions already in hand. Simultaneously, new entrants such as the Liberty Energy–PowerBridge JV are packaging power origination and campus development as an integrated product, compressing the competitive window for independent advisory positioning. Community and regulatory opposition continues to materialize at the local level, as seen with Tract in Virginia, underscoring that interconnection and permitting risk are now as consequential as capital formation risk for greenfield development. The emergence of 16 GW-scale VPP aggregation structures from Tesla, Sunrun, and Renew Home signals that distributed capacity products are maturing into credible offtake alternatives, adding a new layer of structural complexity to PPA negotiations. Across all signals, the common thread is that power origination expertise — spanning generation contracting, interconnection strategy, and regulatory navigation — is becoming the scarcest and most commercially decisive capability in the development stack.

04  Market Signals · context, ranked by relevance
1
ISO-NE
ISO-NE has proposed a rule requiring new large loads to secure their own generation resources and excluding them from the installed capacity requirement.
Airtable  Open record
Read  This proposal, if finalized, would fundamentally shift the burden of capacity procurement from the grid operator to the load developer, making independent generation contracting a prerequisite for interconnection in New England rather than an optional optimization. Data center developers targeting ISO-NE markets would face a structurally higher bar at the queue entry stage, with bespoke generation agreements becoming a gating item rather than a post-interconnection consideration.
Strategic Thought  The BYOG construct creates direct, durable demand for power origination advisory services in the ISO-NE footprint, as developers without in-house generation expertise will need external support to structure compliant resource pairings before submitting interconnection applications. This policy direction also sets a potential precedent that other RTOs and ISOs may monitor and adapt, meaning the ISO-NE signal carries outsized relevance for anticipating queue reform in adjacent markets. Advisors with deep PPA structuring and generation contracting experience in New England are positioned to capture a category of work that did not exist as a standalone requirement before this proposal.
2
Sunrun
Tesla, Sunrun, and Renew Home announced a joint agreement to aggregate rooftop solar, home batteries, and smart thermostats into what may be the largest virtual power plant deployment in the United States, explicitly targeting data center load concentration zones.
Airtable  Open record
Read  The deliberate targeting of data center hot spots signals that distributed capacity aggregators are actively repositioning VPP products as structured offtake alternatives for large commercial and hyperscale loads, not merely retail grid-support tools. This represents a meaningful expansion of the PPA counterparty landscape, introducing aggregated distributed resources as a new asset class in power procurement negotiations alongside conventional utility-scale generation.
Strategic Thought  For power origination advisors, the emergence of credible multi-gigawatt VPP structures as potential offtake products introduces a new layer of contractual and regulatory complexity — distributed resource agreements sit at the intersection of retail tariff structures, aggregation rules, and wholesale market participation frameworks that vary significantly by ISO/RTO. Advisors who can evaluate distributed capacity deals alongside conventional PPAs will have a material edge in helping developer clients stress-test portfolio optionality. The 16 GW scale of this announcement also signals that VPP aggregators are now operating at volumes that make them relevant counterparties in large-load procurement conversations, not just demand response supplements.
3
Tract  VIRGINIA
Data center developer Tract is encountering organized community opposition in Goochland County, Virginia ahead of filing a formal permit application for a new campus.
Airtable  Open record
Read  Community pushback at the pre-application stage is an increasingly common friction point for data center development in Virginia, where permitting timelines and local approval processes have become as consequential as grid queue position in determining project viability. For a developer of Tract's institutional sophistication, the Goochland opposition is notable because it surfaces before the interconnection application is filed, meaning that social license risk and permitting risk are converging earlier in the development cycle than historical norms would suggest.
Strategic Thought  The Virginia community opposition pattern underscores that power origination and interconnection advisory engagements increasingly require parallel fluency in local permitting dynamics, because a project that cannot secure land use approval cannot make productive use of an interconnection queue position. Advisors who can sequence interconnection strategy in coordination with local approval timelines — rather than treating them as independent workstreams — deliver compressibly higher value in contested markets like northern and central Virginia. This signal also reinforces that developers in high-demand markets are facing a widening set of non-technical risks that aggregate around the power and permitting nexus, elevating the strategic importance of integrated advisory support.
4
Liberty Energy, PowerBridge
Liberty Energy and PowerBridge have formed a joint venture to develop powered data center campuses, combining Liberty's distributed power generation capability with PowerBridge's development platform.
Airtable  Open record
Read  The formation of this JV reflects a broader market trend in which power services companies are vertically integrating into campus development to capture value across the full stack, rather than serving purely as infrastructure vendors to developers. For independent power origination advisors, this move compresses the addressable market among developers who might otherwise seek external expertise, but simultaneously signals that power-integrated development is becoming the competitive standard that all market participants must meet or partner to achieve.
Strategic Thought  The Liberty–PowerBridge structure is the latest in a series of vertical integration moves that are reshaping the competitive boundary between power advisory, generation ownership, and site development — a convergence that is narrowing the number of developers who approach power origination as a purely outsourced function. Advisors who operate at the origination and structuring layer rather than the execution or construction layer are partially insulated from this trend, as the highest-value work remains in deal architecture, counterparty negotiation, and regulatory positioning rather than physical asset deployment. The JV's formation also signals that the market is increasingly rewarding advisors and platforms that can demonstrate end-to-end power fluency, not point solutions.
