PowerHouse Hillwood  ILLINOIS
FERC rejected ComEd's attempt to cancel PowerHouse Hillwood's interconnection contract for a 1.8-GW, $20B data center campus in Illinois, affirming the developer's grid access rights.
Airtable  Open record
Read  The ruling preserves PowerHouse Hillwood's interconnection position but exposes the fragility of large-load interconnection agreements when utilities seek unilateral cancellation. Developers at this scale without dedicated regulatory and contract-structuring support face material risk of queue displacement even after agreements are signed.
Strategic Thought  This case signals that interconnection contract enforcement is becoming an active litigation frontier, not merely a transactional formality — particularly for gigawatt-scale load customers in PJM territory. Advisors with FERC-process fluency and contract-structuring depth are increasingly differentiated as utilities push back on large interconnection commitments. The ruling may embolden other large-load developers to contest utility-initiated cancellations, expanding the market for interconnection security advisory.

This dynamic is creating a structural market need for origination and interconnection advisors capable of operating at portfolio breadth, not just project depth.
