Bottom Line
The data center power-procurement and interconnection advisory market is entering a period of elevated regulatory friction across multiple U.S. jurisdictions simultaneously, with Texas pausing certain approvals, Pennsylvania facing environmental litigation, California debating large-load interconnection definitions, and Virginia contending with parallel state and federal proceedings over utility-scale generation. Developers and operators who signed large leases or power agreements in 2024 and early 2025 are now confronting the gap between contractual commitments and actual grid deliverability, placing a premium on structured advisory expertise that bridges FERC, ISO tariff, and state regulatory processes. The hyperscaler-backed build-to-suit segment remains active — Oracle's anchor at Applied Digital's Polaris Forge 2 being a representative example — but the window between lease execution and shovel-ready delivery is compressing as regulatory headwinds mount. Origination and power-structuring advisory demand is rising precisely because in-house development teams at data center operators typically lack the regulatory depth to navigate multi-jurisdictional proceedings concurrently. Market center of gravity today sits at the intersection of stalled interconnection queues, contested generation permitting, and operators who have already made capital commitments but have not yet secured deliverable power.

Airtable  Open record
Read  Parallel state and federal proceedings on the same generation asset create a compounded approval risk where an adverse outcome in either forum can strand the project, illustrating the complexity developers face when utility-scale power solutions involve both ISO/FERC and state commission jurisdictions simultaneously. Strategic Thought  The Chesterfield proceeding is a public case study in the structural complexity of securing new dispatchable generation in PJM's Virginia footprint, the same geography where data center load growth is most acute. For the power-origination advisory market, this signal reinforces that behind-the-meter and bilateral structures that avoid utility generation dependency are gaining relative attractiveness as utility-scale alternatives face extended multi-jurisdictional timelines.
