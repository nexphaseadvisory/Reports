Issue No. 644 · US Data Center Power
The morning read on power-constrained demand.
No new qualifications today, plus 4 market developments worth tracking.
Bottom Line
The U.S. data center power market is consolidating around large-scale, behind-the-meter generation strategies, with AWS's 4.5 GW Homer City filing and Fit Energy's fuel-cell project in Luzerne County both signaling that PJM-territory developers are increasingly bypassing constrained grid interconnection queues in favor of on-site gas-anchored capacity. Simultaneously, the DOE's $4.2 billion conditional loan to Vistra for nuclear uprates across Pennsylvania and Ohio is expanding the pool of firm, 24/7 clean baseload available for structured offtake, creating new PPA origination surface in PJM's western zone. DayOne's U.S. IPO filing, targeting 2.3 GW by 2028 across multiple international markets, underscores that rapidly scaling operators with thin local origination infrastructure represent a growing segment of the advisory market. Across these signals, the common thread is that power procurement complexity — interconnection timing, fuel-cell permitting, nuclear offtake structuring, and cross-border grid navigation — is outpacing in-house capability at both emerging and established data center platforms.

Amazon Web Services  PENNSYLVANIA
AWS filed plans for a 36-building data center campus on approximately 1,100 acres at the Homer City Energy Campus in Indiana County, Pennsylvania, anchored by a 4.5 GW behind-the-meter gas generation plant with over 1 GW of planned grid injection.
Airtable  Open record
Read  The scale of the Homer City filing — combining behind-the-meter generation with meaningful grid export — signals that hyperscalers are now designing campuses that function simultaneously as load and as generation assets within PJM, compressing the distinction between offtaker and generator. This dual-role structure creates layered complexity across interconnection queue positioning, PPA counterparty identification for the export capacity, and FERC jurisdictional treatment that few advisors are positioned to navigate end-to-end.
Strategic Thought  When a single campus project carries both a large generation interconnection and a load interconnection component in PJM, the origination and structuring work fragments across multiple queue processes, capacity market filings, and potentially separate transmission service agreements — each with its own timeline and risk surface. This dynamic is expanding the addressable scope of interconnection advisory engagements well beyond traditional load-side consulting. Advisors with fluency in both generator and load interconnection under PJM's tariff, combined with PPA structuring experience on the offtake side of the exported capacity, occupy a narrow and increasingly valuable position in the market.

Vistra  PENNSYLVANIA
The U.S. Department of Energy issued a conditional commitment for a $4.2 billion loan to Vistra to fund nuclear uprates and modernization at Beaver Valley in Pennsylvania and Davis-Besse and Perry in Ohio, preserving approximately 4 GW of existing capacity and adding 433 MW.
Airtable  Open record
Read  Federal capital flowing into PJM nuclear baseload at this scale materially improves the creditworthiness and longevity of Vistra's generation fleet, making these plants more viable PPA counterparties for data center operators seeking long-term, 24/7 carbon-free power contracts. The timing aligns with a wave of hyperscaler and colocation operator demand for firm clean power in the PJM footprint, where new renewable interconnection timelines remain extended.
Strategic Thought  Nuclear PPAs structured against uprated, federally-backed capacity carry a different risk profile than merchant or greenfield nuclear agreements, potentially unlocking offtake interest from data center operators whose treasury and ESG teams require higher credit certainty. The interconnection advisory angle centers on matching that expanded baseload supply to load-side customers navigating PJM capacity obligations and delivery point selection — a structuring exercise that requires simultaneous fluency in generation asset positioning and load interconnection. As more nuclear capacity enters this structured-offtake market, the intermediary function between generator and data center load becomes a distinct and recurring advisory service.

DayOne
Singapore-based DayOne filed for a U.S. IPO while targeting 2.3 GW of total capacity by 2028, with 962 MW currently live and 1.3 GW under construction across APAC, Finland, and Spain.
Airtable  Open record
Read  DayOne's geographic expansion into Finland and Spain — both markets with distinct grid regulatory regimes, capacity mechanisms, and interconnection processes — creates immediate gaps in local power-procurement and grid-access expertise that an APAC-headquartered operator is structurally unlikely to fill internally at pace. The IPO filing adds a capital-markets dimension that will pressure the platform to demonstrate credible power-delivery timelines to public investors, raising the stakes on origination execution in new markets.
Strategic Thought  Operators scaling rapidly across multiple international grid regimes face compounding origination risk: each new market introduces a distinct interconnection authority, regulatory body, and PPA counterparty universe that must be navigated concurrently with construction timelines. This multi-jurisdiction pressure is creating demand for advisory capability that is both market-specific and cross-border in its structuring literacy. The intersection of IPO-driven disclosure requirements and power-delivery execution risk is an increasingly common trigger for data center platforms to seek outside origination and structuring support rather than build internal teams from scratch.
4
Fit Energy  PENNSYLVANIA
Fit Energy received zoning approval and has permitting underway for a natural gas fuel-cell-powered data center in Newport Township, Luzerne County, Pennsylvania, utilizing FuelCell Energy units.
Airtable  Open record
Read  A fuel-cell-anchored behind-the-meter data center in PJM-territory Pennsylvania sits at the intersection of distributed generation permitting, FERC small-power-producer qualification considerations, and air-quality regulatory review — a combination that creates multi-layered origination and structuring complexity at a scale that early-stage developers rarely have in-house. Zoning approval with permitting still underway represents the window in which power strategy, interconnection posture, and any grid-export optionality are most efficiently defined.
Strategic Thought  Fuel-cell generation at data center scale occupies an ambiguous regulatory position: depending on interconnection size and export intent, projects may interface with FERC's small generator interconnection procedures, state utility commission jurisdiction, or both, while simultaneously managing fuel-supply contracting and emissions permitting under state environmental frameworks. Developers at this stage frequently lack the origination infrastructure to optimize across those overlapping processes, and decisions made during the permitting window — particularly on interconnection application timing and capacity market participation — carry long-term cost and timeline consequences. The Luzerne County project is an early indicator of a broader market segment: behind-the-meter fuel-cell data centers in PJM that will need structured advisory support as the technology scales.
